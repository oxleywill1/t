Clearing granny flats, units and small spaces

Big cleanouts · 6 minute read

Small dwellings are deceptive. A one-bedroom unit or granny flat that looks manageable frequently produces more volume than a family home's spare rooms, because everything the occupant owned is compressed into it.

Access is the main variable

In houses, access is usually straightforward. In units and flats it rarely is:

  • Lifts. Many buildings require lift bookings, and some require protective padding installed by building management. This can take days to arrange, so start early.
  • Loading docks and time windows. Buildings often restrict removals to weekday business hours, which can conflict with settlement dates.
  • Stairs. Walk-up blocks mean every item is carried, which is the largest labour factor in any quote.
  • Parking. Where there's no dock or driveway, a truck may need a permit to stand in a loading zone or on the street.
  • Narrow corridors and tight turns. Some items that went in during construction won't come out through a corridor and need dismantling.

Tell whoever quotes the job about all of this upfront. A quote given without knowing about a third-floor walk-up will be revised on the day.

Strata and body corporate rules

Most buildings have by-laws covering removals: permitted hours, notification requirements, insurance certificates from contractors, and restrictions on using common area bins. Common area bins in particular are not for clear-out volumes — filling them with a unit's contents is both a by-law breach and unfair to other residents.

Check with the building manager or strata committee before booking, and ask what documentation your contractor needs to provide.

Granny flats and secondary dwellings

These are often cleared after an elderly relative has moved into care or passed away, which makes them emotionally loaded jobs as well as practical ones. They also tend to contain a lifetime compressed into a small space — decades of photographs, documents and keepsakes in a single room.

Treat the document and photograph search as its own task, done before any bulk clearing. The volume is small enough that a careful search is genuinely feasible, unlike a full house.

Storage cages and garages

Nearly every unit comes with a storage cage or allocated garage space, and they're routinely forgotten until after the main clearance is booked. Check the strata plan or ask the building manager which space belongs to the unit — cages are frequently unlabelled and occupants sometimes used a different one than the one allocated.

Practical sequence

Book the lift or dock first, since it's the constraint everything else works around. Then the clearance, then cleaning, then the final inspection or handover. Where a sale or lease deadline is involved, build in a buffer day — building access restrictions cause more missed deadlines in units than any other factor.

Office and commercial cleanouts

Big cleanouts · 7 minute read

Commercial clearances have constraints residential ones don't: lease obligations, data security, building access rules and the fact that the work usually can't disrupt trading.

Start with the lease

Most commercial leases contain a make-good clause requiring the tenant to return the premises to a defined condition at the end of the term. What that means varies enormously — sometimes just removing your fit-out, sometimes restoring to base building shell, sometimes repainting and recarpeting.

Read the clause early, because it determines the scope of the whole job. Disputes over make-good are among the most common commercial tenancy disagreements, and the cost difference between interpretations can be substantial. Where the wording is ambiguous, get advice before you start removing things.

Data destruction is not optional

Anything holding data needs handling separately: computers, servers, phones, photocopiers and printers with internal storage, and paper records. Under the Privacy Act, organisations have obligations to destroy or de-identify personal information they no longer need, and a photocopier sold with its drive intact is a genuine breach risk.

For paper records, secure destruction with a certificate of destruction is the standard approach. For hardware, either certified data destruction or physical drive removal before the equipment leaves the building. Don't let equipment go out the door on the assumption someone downstream will handle it.

Furniture: the awkward truth

Used office furniture has very little resale value, and the market is thin. Workstations in particular are difficult to move on because they're system-specific and labour-intensive to dismantle. Options worth trying before disposal:

  • Commercial furniture dealers, who sometimes take quality chairs and desks
  • Charities, community groups, schools and not-for-profits, many of which will take good desks and chairs
  • Social enterprises that refurbish office furniture
  • Staff, who will often take chairs and small items

Start these conversations weeks ahead. Nobody collects furniture at two days' notice.

Access and timing

Most commercial buildings restrict removal work to after hours or weekends, require dock bookings, and need contractor insurance certificates and sometimes inductions lodged in advance. Lift access for large items may need the goods lift booked and padded. Build all of this into the timeline — it's usually the constraint that sets the schedule, not the work itself.

What to separate

Commercial loads separate well, which reduces cost: e-waste, paper and cardboard, scrap metal from shelving and partitions, timber, and general. Fluorescent tubes, batteries and any chemicals from a workshop or kitchen need the hazardous waste route.

Documentation

For a make-good, photograph the premises before and after, and keep disposal dockets. Where a bond or bank guarantee is at stake, that evidence is what resolves disagreements with the landlord.
